Case 03 · Market entry & partnerships

Entering the UAE from India, with a pipeline to show for it

Entering a market from outside it is a trust problem before it is a pipeline problem. Partners, spec work and a small paid pilot all do the same job: they let a buyer test you cheaply.

Input · where it started

A well-regarded Indian agency with international ambition and ad hoc business development. Occasional conversations, no market map, no repeatable process.

Output · where it ended

A named list of the accounts, partners and decision makers worth calling in the UAE, and a system for calling them that keeps running after the consultant leaves.

The situation

The company already had a good reputation in India, but there was no proper plan for international expansion. Most business development was happening in an ad hoc way. The brief I set was to build a growth engine rather than to find a few clients, because finding a few clients solves one quarter and a system solves the market.

The market map

I started with research, and made it specific enough to act on:

  • Which countries actually made sense to enter first, and which industries to target.
  • What the open briefs in the UAE market were at that moment.
  • Which partners were already operating in the market and worth working with.
  • Which Shopify companies operating in the UAE could become partners rather than prospects.
  • Who the real decision makers were, by name.

From that I built the go-to-market strategy, defined the ICPs, shortlisted target accounts and put together a revenue plan, so that every activity mapped back to a business goal rather than to activity for its own sake. The deliverables were operational: contact data for consultants, LinkedIn outreach automation running through AI agents, contacts for Shopify app partners we could work with on a client-by-client basis, and a list of UAE agencies with multi-country clients where the relationship could run both ways. They could deliver for us in the UAE and we could deliver for them in India.

Partnerships as the primary channel

A large part of the work was partner-led rather than direct. I researched Shopify partners, SaaS companies, AI platforms, consultants and agencies across the UAE and MENA that could become referral or co-selling partners, on the view that an unknown agency entering a new market borrows trust faster than it builds it.

The outbound system

Alongside that I built the outbound process end to end: LinkedIn outreach, email sequences, WhatsApp follow-ups, discovery calls, case studies, proposals, pilot projects, and the conversion of those pilots into long-term retainers. To make it repeatable I also built the sales playbooks, case studies, outreach templates, meeting trackers, pipeline reports and weekly execution plans, so the team was not starting from a blank page every week.

The goal was never lead generation. It was a repeatable international business development model the company could keep using in the next market.

Two worked examples

A regional super app

We had been tracking a head of growth at a large Indian quick-commerce brand. He moved to the super app as head of marketing, and that relationship gave us the meeting. We proposed starting with a pilot and pitched several products: app store optimisation for downloads, AI search visibility so that the brand surfaces when someone asks an AI assistant which company is best in Dubai, plus SEO and performance marketing. Performance marketing and AI search visibility were what landed. It closed on a monthly retainer with managed spend.

A UAE bank

Once the first account was cracked we moved immediately, while we had proof. I found the contact through LinkedIn automation, started with a marketing executive, and he introduced the head of marketing. We could not get a slot, so instead of chasing it we sent examples of our work, and then, with Eid approaching, built spec work for the bank rather than asking for more time. That converted into a pilot on a single service, creatives plus performance, running around 50 days and structured to move onto a retainer afterwards. We also introduced partners with a global presence and supported website optimisation work.

The commercial model

Revenue was a retainer plus a percentage of managed media spend. As the account grows, through higher ROAS, more purchase orders, more impressions, lower CAC and higher AOV, the spend grows and the agency's revenue grows with it. That structure aligns the agency to the client's performance rather than to the invoice, and it is the reason pilots were priced to convert rather than to maximise the first cheque.

Where it landed

  • Two flagship UAE accounts onboarded inside the engagement, a regional super app and a bank, both starting as pilots structured to become retainers.
  • Deals in the $20K to $150K+ range closed and managed across UAE, KSA, Qatar, Kuwait, Bahrain and North America.
  • A market-entry model, partner map and outbound system the company retained after the engagement ended.

The two accounts above are the most recent examples. Other engagements in other markets are not described here.

What I would carry into the next one

The spec work built around Eid closed a bank that would not give us a meeting. That is a better argument for doing the work upfront than any follow-up sequence I could write.
